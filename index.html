Cap-Weighted (SPY) vs Equal-Weighted (RSP) Divergence

💡 What This Divergence Means

The S&P 500 (SPY) is a market-cap weighted index, meaning a 1% move in Apple or Nvidia has more impact than a 1% move in 100 small constituents combined. In contrast, Invesco S&P 500 Equal Weight (RSP) assigns an equal 0.2% weight to every company.

When SPY dramatically outpaces RSP, it signals narrow market breadth: the headline index looks strong, but the median American stock is lagging behind. This phenomenon preceded major market corrections in 1973 and 2000.

⚠️ What is Equity Risk Premium (ERP)?

ERP is the extra yield investors receive for holding risky equities over risk-free 10-year US Treasury bonds:

ERP = (1 / S&P 500 P/E) − 10-Year Treasury Yield

When ERP is below 1%, stocks offer virtually zero risk premium compared to guaranteed government bonds. Investors take on high equity volatility with little compensatory return.

🧮 Equity Duration Formula

Just like bonds, equity duration measures percentage price change per 100 bp yield shift: ΔP / P ≈ −D_equity · Δy + ΔEarnings. A 100 bp increase in long-term rates implies a ~25% to 30% contraction in justified P/E multiples unless offset by explosive earnings revisions.

Key Financial Concepts

1. Market Breadth (市场广度)

The percentage of index components participating in an advance. High index levels paired with declining breadth (fewer stocks making new highs) historically foreshadows market fragility.

2. Equity Risk Premium (ERP, 股权风险溢价)

The excess return earned above risk-free U.S. government debt. When ERP falls below historical norms (e.g. <1.0%), capital typically rotates out of high-multiple equities into fixed income.

3. Equity Duration (股票久期)

A measure of equity price sensitivity to interest rate fluctuations. High-growth tech companies with cash flows expected 15-30 years out behave like long-duration zero-coupon bonds.

4. Value at Risk (VaR, 在险价值)

The maximum loss expected over a given holding period at a specified confidence level (e.g., 95% 1-year VaR of -28% means 19 out of 20 years losses won't exceed 28%).
